# Protein family evolution, structural interpretation, and assay claims

- Profile models describe position-specific frequencies; Potts models add pairwise residue couplings. Neither automatically defines a calibrated organismal fitness landscape without explicit assumptions and checks.
- Protein family membership must consider homology, domains, aligned positions, paralogs and divergent subfamilies; include negative/family-external controls when claiming specificity.
- Use consistent numbering across a reference sequence, multiple sequence alignment, structure chain and engineered variants. Explicitly list unresolved, deleted or ambiguous residues.
- Distinguish substitution rate shifts, lineage-specific constraint changes, episodic selection, convergent motif changes, and measured activity; they are not synonyms.
- For microbial rhodopsins, proton/sodium/chloride transport types require experimental or rigorously calibrated functional evidence; short motif-based labels can be uncertain.
- In-silico mutation or structural stability scores can prioritize test hypotheses but cannot confirm ion transport, membrane localization, expression, stress response, or desert adaptation.
- A functional transport assay in an expression system supports the tested biochemical activity only. Organismal stress fitness must be tested with relevant conditions, native genetic context or appropriate follow-up evidence.
